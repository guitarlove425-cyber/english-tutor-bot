# Curriculum redesign research notes

## Sources and verified principles

1. Council of Europe, CEFR Companion Volume (2020): the framework covers reception, production, interaction, and mediation; it also describes linguistic, sociolinguistic, and pragmatic competences, including phonological control and fluency. Source: https://rm.coe.int/common-european-framework-of-reference-for-languages-learning-teaching/16809ea0d4
2. Council of Europe, The action-oriented approach: learning should be organised through realistic, unifying scenarios spanning several lessons and leading to a final collaborative task or project. Scenarios should include reception, production, interaction, and mediation, with teacher input, support, autonomy, authenticity, and self-assessment. Source: https://www.coe.int/en/web/common-european-framework-reference-languages/the-action-oriented-approach
3. Cambridge Primary English: the curriculum integrates reading, writing, speaking, and listening; it uses clear learning objectives, authentic contexts, broad activity types, and progression tests/Checkpoint-style milestones. Source: https://www.cambridgeinternational.org/programmes-and-qualifications/cambridge-primary/curriculum/english/
4. Cambridge English Integrated Learning and Assessment: teaching, learning, and assessment should be integrated so teachers can measure progress, guide learners, and provide actionable feedback. Cambridge describes CEFR-aligned journeys, regular assessment touchpoints, and level-appropriate learning solutions. Source: https://www.cambridge.org/cambridgeenglish/integrated-learning-and-assessment
5. Cambridge English school qualifications brochure: learning journeys use CEFR milestones and authentic situations across speaking, writing, reading, and listening; assessment should show progress and what learners should do next. Source: https://www.cambridgeenglish.org/Images/469350-cambridge-english-qualifications-schools-brochure.pdf
6. British Council TeachingEnglish resources: lesson plans are organised by CEFR levels, ages, topics, and durations; examples include staged topic projects and follow-up tasks. Source: https://www.teachingenglish.org.uk/teaching-resources
7. British Council task-based learning: meaningful communication is organised around a task, followed by language analysis and practice. The documented task-based sequence includes pre-task, task, planning, report, analysis, and practice. Source: https://www.teachingenglish.org.uk/professional-development/teachers/teaching-knowledge-database/t-w/tbl-task-based-learning
8. British Council, A task-based approach: PPP can introduce and practise target language, while task-based work supports authentic communication; a blended approach is appropriate for structured curricula and beginners. The TBL sequence includes pre-task, task, planning, report, analysis, and practice. Source: https://www.teachingenglish.org.uk/professional-development/teachers/knowing-subject/task-based-approach

## Design decisions for this repository

- Keep explicit, Burmese-first teacher explanation and controlled practice for beginners.
- Add action-oriented real-life scenarios and projects for higher levels.
- Treat reception, production, interaction, and mediation as separate skill targets rather than repeating the same speaking task in every feature.
- Keep Academy as the sequenced core language curriculum; make Beginner a shorter bridge; make Kids age-appropriate and phonics/story/project centred.
- Make Quiz, Coach, Diagnostic, Word Bank, Pronunciation, Live Voice, Role-play, Projects, and Classroom practice draw from separate task banks and measurable outcomes rather than pretending to be duplicate lessons.
- Use regular low-stakes checks and mastery evidence, with remediation and a clear next action.
- Write original content inspired by the frameworks; do not copy copyrighted coursebook wording or lesson text.
